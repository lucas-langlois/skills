# Sections and paragraph flow

## Match the document to its purpose

Use the target template and article type. A technical report needs context and practical interpretation for its intended users; a research article needs its journal's format and argument. Apply systematic-review or other reporting standards only when the study design warrants them.

| Section | Content to establish | Scientist input when missing | Failure to flag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Title | Subject, scope and useful specificity | Preferred emphasis and exact system/geography | Promotional novelty or vague scope |
| NESP executive summary | Four template parts: problem; solution/objectives; value; importance/impact. Embed verified findings and relevant limitations so it stands alone | Main outcome and practical implication in the author's words | Future benefits described as demonstrated results |
| Journal abstract | Required format/length; question, approach, principal results and bounded conclusion | Main finding and takeaway | Numbers or conclusions differing from the paper |
| Introduction | Relevant context, known evidence, specific uncertainty/gap and objectives/questions | Actual scientific/management question | Broad generic opening, unsupported 'first', literature list without synthesis |
| Methods | What was actually done, where/when, design, sampling, processing, analyses and assumptions | Missing procedures, exclusions, deviations and reasons | Invented workflow; reported analyses absent from Methods |
| Results | Findings in objective order, units, denominators, uncertainty and figure/table links | Final outputs and meaning of each number | Interpretation presented as measurement; ambiguous sample size |
| Discussion | Answer, interpretation, evidence comparisons, alternatives, limitations and applicability | Author explanation, unexpected results and strongest limitation | Repeated results; unsupported mechanisms; overgeneralisation |
| Conclusions | Supported answers, scope and meaningful limitations | What the scientist is prepared to conclude | New results or unsupported claims |
| Recommendations | Evidence-linked action for an identified user, priority and conditions if known | Who should do what and why | Vague 'more research'; unsupported prescriptions |
| References | All cited sources and verified metadata in the selected style | Relevant library/output style and ambiguous identities | Invented DOI or wrong source attached to a claim |
| Appendices | Supporting methods, data dictionaries, extended results and protocols, with main-text callouts | Material needed for reproducibility/use | Orphan material; essential caveats hidden from the main text |
| Declarations | Actual funding, contributions, conflicts, permissions, data/code, ethics and AI statements where required | Confirmed author statements and venue instructions | Invented approvals, roles or commitments |

Discussion is a useful optional addition to the NESP template for analytical studies, not a mandated heading. Agree the placement of Recommendations for the audience. Write the summary after the body is coherent, then cross-check all key numbers and conclusions.

## Reverse outline and flow

Give each paragraph a short purpose label: context, gap, operation, result, interpretation, qualification or recommendation. Keep these labels in the editing notes, not the manuscript.

Check that each paragraph contributes something distinct, each sentence supports its purpose and the next paragraph follows for an identifiable reason. Merge duplication, split unrelated jobs and move misplaced detail. Do not impose identical sentence counts or paragraph patterns.

Move from information already introduced to the next idea. Prefer specific relationships to filler transitions. Repeating a technical term can improve precision. When a logical link is missing, ask the scientist for it. Do not infer causality from chronology or correlation, or replace a strong unsupported claim with a weaker unsupported claim and call it verified.

## Ecology and remote-sensing checks

Check only relevant distinctions using supplied evidence:

- Unit of inference: pixel, tile, quadrat, transect, site or survey; independent sampling units and dependence where known.
- Held-out validation versus training/tuning; possible leakage as a query when the split is unclear.
- Reproducibility details when applicable: model/version, input resolution, tiling/overlap, threshold, aggregation rule, processing and software.
- Separate presence, cover, species, habitat area and count; identify reference standard, units, uncertainty and class-specific metrics where available.
- Separate ground sampling distance, geolocation accuracy and detectability. Mapped non-detection does not establish true absence by itself.
- Limit transfer claims to conditions supported by the design or external validation.

These are editorial checks, not permission to invent metadata, run unrequested analyses, change Methods or replace the scientist's judgement.
