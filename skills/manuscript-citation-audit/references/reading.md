# Targeted full-text verification

## Find evidence without selecting only favorable passages

1. Restate the manuscript sentence as precise checkable propositions, preserving numbers, causal language, comparisons, population, habitat and scope. Identify which proposition each citation is intended to support.
2. Orient from the original article's title, abstract and headings, then search its full text for concepts, synonyms, spelling variants, abbreviations, quantities and units. A failed exact-word search is not a failed citation. Check extraction quality before interpreting zero hits.
3. Read each promising passage with preceding/following paragraphs and enough section context to identify what was measured, by whom and under what conditions. Follow cross-references to relevant methods, results, tables, figures or supplements. Verify denominators and experimental design behind numerical claims; a discussion sentence alone may omit those limits.
4. Inspect applicable limitations and conclusions, and search elsewhere in the article for exceptions, contrary results and qualifications relevant to the same proposition. Check whether a passage reports this article's evidence, an earlier paper's findings or speculation. For synthesis/review claims, assess whether the cited review actually makes the synthesis; do not claim its underlying primary studies were inspected unless they were.
5. Revisit the decisive evidence against the exact manuscript wording. Record source locators, short evidence, reasoning and material limits, including each source's contribution to a grouped citation.

## When to broaden reading

Expand beyond targeted passages when the sentence summarizes the paper broadly, asserts a general recommendation or absence of evidence, combines findings across sections, depends on mixed results or an unfamiliar method, or has ambiguous terminology/version differences. Read the complete article when these issues cannot be resolved from targeted sections. Inspect claim-relevant supplementary material when the article delegates decisive methods/results there.

Do not label a claim unsupported just because a search found no match. Broaden the search and inspect plausible sections. A negative attribution verdict requires enough coverage to establish that the source does not substantiate the claim; otherwise use `unverifiable` with the gap explained. A clear conflicting result may justify `contradicted` without cover-to-cover reading once its scope and applicable qualifications have been checked.

## Completion criteria

A targeted claim-level check is complete when source identity/version is reliable, decisive original full-text evidence has been inspected, necessary methods/results and applicable limitations are understood, relevant conflicting evidence/qualifications have been sought, and no unresolved access or interpretation gap could materially change that verdict. Record the actual inspected sections/pages. This does not assert exhaustive reading or guarantee that no issue was missed.

`partial` means the inspected source supports only part or a narrower version of the claim; it does not mean the agent has merely read part of the article. Reading a subset can complete a well-supported targeted check. Conversely, reading every page does not automatically make a scientifically ambiguous check complete.

HTML and PDF are equally acceptable evidence formats when sufficiently complete and readable. PDF downloads, whole-document extraction and search indexes do not demonstrate inspection. For truncated tool output, request bounded sections/page ranges until the necessary evidence is actually visible. If context is tight, save concise evidence notes with locators and resume from them; do not replace original-source checking with a generated summary. Keep one ledger per work/version, linking its inspected sections and relevant gaps to each claim ID.
