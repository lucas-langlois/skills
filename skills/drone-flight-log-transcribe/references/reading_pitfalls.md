# Reading pitfalls (from the Oct 2026 Tiwi job)

These were misreadings in a first-pass transcription, caught by zooming in or by a second reviewer.

## Handwriting
- **7 read as 2 in altitude.** "37 m" was read as "32 m" on two days. This writer's 7 has a crossbar and a short tail. Zoom every altitude cell. A 2 has a flat base stroke ("24 m" at Kerleske).
- **5 vs 3 in minutes.** 11:05 was read as 11:03 and 10:45 as 10:43. Compare with a known "25" on the same sheet.
- **25 vs 23** for stop battery: ambiguous on some sheets. Flag it red.
- **"100" written as "l00" or "600"**: the 1 has a closed loop. It is 100.
- **Plural and verb endings**: "extras", not "extra"; "Redid 44", not "Redo 44". Read words letter by letter.
- **Place names**: write the spelling exactly as it appears ("Timasambu") and flag it red. Don't swap in a familiar name.

## Overwrites and crossings-out
- A bold digit written over another (e.g. a 1 over a 4 in "11:14") is the final value. Record the bold digit, flag it red, and give the other reading.
- A pilot cell with "AN" scribbled out and "KW" written beneath means KW. The dittos below then also mean KW.
- A struck letter followed by a ditto (`A̶ "`) means "same as above", e.g. AN + Tiwi Rangers.
- A crossed-out time with a new time below: use the new time and list the change in appendix notes.

## Cross-checks that resolve doubts
- A start time must come after the previous flight's end. Kerleske R4 at 11:40 fits after R5 ended at 11:38; 12:40 would come after R4's own 12:02 end.
- Flight length vs battery: about 25 min for 100 → 20-25 %. A short flight ending with 70 % left means an aborted or partial flight.
- **Spot ranges continue and wrap round**: 10-25 → 26-49 → 50-09 means 50 to the end, then 1-9. Don't "correct" 50-09 to 50-89. Unsuccessful numbers often fill the gap (56-78 unsuccessful sits beside 79-89 completed).
- Dates: a sheet dated 28/10/2020 inside a 2026 batch is a slip. Record 2026 and note it.

## Structure
- Spot-check sheets carry no date or site. Pair each with the ortho sheet photographed just before it.
- One day can have two ortho sheets (two sites) but only one spot sheet. The spot sheet belongs to the site shot just before it. A note like "+ spot checks" on an ortho flight does not mean a separate sheet exists.
- Different aircraft (Mavic 3T "Tiwi", Mavic 3/3E "Rooster") can fly ortho and spot checks at the same time. Battery prefixes (R*, TB*) don't always match the aircraft name.
